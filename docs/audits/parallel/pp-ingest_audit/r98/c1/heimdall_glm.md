### 1.2 The cap (question 2)

So the overshoot bound is: each admitted call can cost at most (its actual cost − 0.25) more than reserved. For Gemini with a large prompt (the mining prompt with 40 sentences ≈ 2,400-9,000 tokens → 9,000 × 5/1e6 = $0.045) plus 4,000 output = $0.10 → ~$0.145, well under 0.25. So a single Gemini call admitted at spent=1.80, reserved=0 → 1.80+0.25 ≤ 2.00 → admitted; it then costs up to ~$0.445 → spent = 2.245, overshoot $0.245. **The bound is: overshoot ≤ (actual cost − reserve) per concurrently-admitted call, and the last admitted call's full cost beyond the cap.** With attempts=2 (providers.json:114) and the retry loop in `_try` (providers.py:917), *each attempt* adds its cost via `self.caps.add(role, r.cost_usd)` (providers.py:933-934, 950-951) — but the reservation is taken **once per `run()` call, per back-end, in the `for bid in chain` loop** (providers.py:859-862) and released in the `finally` (providers.py:873-875). So: reserve 0.25 → attempt 1 costs $0.40 (added) → attempt 2 (invalid reply, retried) costs another $0.40 (added) → total added 0.80 against a 0.25 reservation. **Overshoot bound: (attempts × per-call cost) − reserve for the last admitted call.** With attempts=2 and Gemini's 16k floor, worst case ≈ 2×0.445 − 0.25 = $0.64 past the cap. The ruling says "Cap $2.00 vendor-reported per day... then queue and wait" — a $2.64 actual spend is a 32% overshoot. Not a violation (the ruling's cap is on *admission*, and the note at providers.json:117 says "with cap_reserve_usd held for every call in flight so the calls never start past it" — which is literally true: no call *starts* past it), but the note's "so the calls never start past it" is the only guarantee; the *day's* spend can exceed $2.00. Worth stating.

### 1.9 Summary of correctness

The diff is largely correct. The genuinely notable items:
- **F6/F7**: the cap's overshoot bound (attempts × per-call cost − reserve; Gemini's 16k `max_tokens_floor` (providers.json:73) makes a single call cost up to ~$0.445 against a $0.25 reserve, and `attempts: 2` (providers.json:114) doubles it) — the *day's* spend can exceed $2.00 by ~$0.64. ## SECTION 6: WORLD-CLASS GAP ANALYSIS

**Genuinely excellent (no change needed):**
- The ledger's append-only discipline with the flock, the torn-tail seal, the pre-write validation, and the mined.at stamping under the lock (ledger.py:34-66) — this is production-grade.
- The `carry_forward` three-valued flag logic (ledger.py:187-209) with its explanatory comment and dedicated tests — rare care.
- The breaker/throttle/budget/cap layering in the Router, each independently testable, with the *outage-vs-rejected* distinction (providers.py:62-70) that the whole retry design hangs on.
- The STATE-notice top-block protocol (spend.py:35-63): multiple independent writers, one lock, each replacing only its own lines, the switch line pinned first. This is the hardest part of a headless multi-writer notice board, and it's right.

**Material gaps:**

1. **No observability into *why* the route did not open.** When the signal is plan_b but the failure clock is < 20 min, or the state is unreadable, nothing is recorded. `live.json`'s `paid_route.open: false` + `recorded.open: false` tells you *that*, not *why* (12:40 into the outage? signal unread?). One event per state *change* of the *pre-conditions* (e.g. `paid_route_wait: failing_since 12:40, need 20:00`) would make the 20-minute window debuggable at 3am. (downsignal.py:210-216)

2. **The cap's overshoot is unbounded in the note's own terms.** The note (providers.json:117) promises "the calls never start past it" — true — but the ruling's number is "$2.00 vendor-reported per day". With `attempts: 2` (providers.json:114) and Gemini's 16k `max_tokens_floor` (providers.json:73), the day's spend can reach ~$2.64. Either reserve the *expected* cost (a per-back-end `cost_estimate_usd` in the config) or record the overshoot in the cap notice. (providers.py:843, 859-862; providers.json:73, 114, 116)

3. **The paid route's rows are invisible to the ruling-40 precision check's *denominator* semantics.** `flagcheck.sample` (flagcheck.py:223-238) samples by `published_at` — a paid-row mined at hour 20 of a 12-h-expiry... no, published_at is the video's. Fine. But: a paid row that is *re-mined* on Heimdall produces a *new* line whose `mined.model` is Heimdall's — the *paid* model's labels are then only in the earlier line. The precision study (flagcheck) reads the *latest* line (flagcheck.py:212-220) — so the paid model's precision is silently *not measured* once the recheck lands. If the orchestrator wants the paid route's labels audited (the ruling: "so its labels can be re-checked"), the study should stratify by `miner_route`. It doesn't (flagcheck.py:589-594 has no miner_route in the results). (flagcheck.py:589-594; mine.py:439)

4. **No end-to-end test of the *cap* under concurrency.** `test_paid_route.py::test_only_the_freeze_lifted_role_calls_a_frozen_vendor_and_never_starts_a_call_past_its_cap` exists, but the overshoot bound (F6) — attempts × cost vs reserve — is the interesting invariant and is not asserted. A test that runs 3 concurrent videos × 2 attempts against a $2 cap with a known per-call cost, and asserts `spent ≤ cap + (attempts × cost − reserve)`, would pin the actual guarantee. (tests/ingest/test_paid_route.py)

5. **`_route_tick`'s naive-datetime risk** (F1) — one `config.aware()`-style guard (or a documented assumption A33) would close it. (downsignal.py:211-215)
