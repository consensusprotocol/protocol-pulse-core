## 1. WHAT THEY CAUGHT THAT I MISSED

**1.1 (muse, C4/C5 — the `_conv` cache and the 200-row window).** I noted the sort in `_paid_due` but did not cost it. Muse's reading is right and worth stating precisely: `service.py:562` filters `self.q.due(Q.MINE_DUE, 200)` to `published_ts >= now - new_within_s`, then `conv(v)` (service.py:566-576) reads `canonical_transcript(vid)` — a full transcript file — once per *new* video, inside the sort key, on the mine thread, every 5 s. The cache makes it once per video *ever* (until the 5000 clear), so the steady-state cost is one read per new upload; the 5000-clear (service.py:563-564) is a blunt but bounded reset. I called this "correct" in Cycle 1 and I still do; what I missed was the *interaction* muse's C4 implies: because `due()` orders by `COALESCE(published_ts, listing_ts, discovered_ts) DESC` (queue.py:178-180), the 200-row window is the 200 *newest* due rows, so during a long outage the new uploads (which the paid route needs) are always inside it. Muse reached the same conclusion ("not a bug given ordering") — but only after almost calling it a bug. I never even raised it. Their instinct was better than mine.

**1.2 (qwen, the reserve/add split).** Qwen's "race" framing was wrong (both `reserve` and `add` take `self.lock`; there is no interleaving) — and the consensus already said so — but qwen's *sub-finding* was the real one and I missed it: the reservation bounds the *start* of a call, not its *cost*. In the pre-fix code the flat $0.25 reserve was smaller than one Gemini call's worst case (~$0.83: 2 attempts × its 16,000-token floor at $25/MTok out), so the day could end past $2.00. The fix (the priced worst case, `Router.run` providers.py:859-860 + `_worst_cost` providers.py:885-890) is exactly the right shape: `need = max(reserve, worst_case)` and no call starts unless its worst case fits. I had Finding 1 (the naive-clock risk) as my headline; qwen's cap-overshoot was the more consequential defect and I gave it one clause. Honest: they contributed more.

**1.3 (muse, R1–R4).** Muse's concurrency table is more thorough than anything I wrote. R1 (plain bool, GIL-atomic) and R3 (the flip mid-batch completes on the old route; the next `mine_once` picks the new one) are exactly right and I never wrote them down. R4 — `_queue_rechecks` (service.py:580-590) does `set_state(TRANSCRIBED)` directly, not via `claim` — is a real subtlety: it is safe *only* because the service lock (service.py:132-146) guarantees one process and the mine thread is the only caller. I agree with their verdict and missed the analysis.

**1.4 (qwen, the `recheck_due` 11-hour question).** Qwen asked whether a video mined on the paid route 11 h ago gets re-mined when the signal flips back, and answered "intended (A31)". Correct: `recheck_due` (queue.py:188-193) filters `published_ts >= now - horizon_s` (12 h) and `recheck_ts IS NULL`, so a video published 11 h ago is inside the horizon and *should* be re-mined — its moments expire at 12 h, so this is the last chance. But qwen's question exposes something neither of us stated: the re-mine itself takes *time*. A video published at T is re-queued at, say, T+11.9 h; the Heimdall pass then runs minutes later, possibly after T+12 h. Its *rows* are still written (the ledger keeps them; expiry is judged at read time by the adapter, ledger.py:336-338), so nothing is lost — the re-mine's flags simply arrive for moments that are already expired. That is the documented "receipt" behavior (rulings 76+77), not a loss. I agree with qwen's conclusion; the sharper statement is that the 12 h horizon bounds the *queueing*, not the *completion*, and the design absorbs that.

**1.5 (muse, C6 — the cap trips early by estimate).** Right and I under-called it: Gemini's cost is the price-table estimate (providers.json:75, "estimate"), which the note itself calls an upper bound, so `caps.add` (providers.py:716-719) can push `spent` past what xAI's own reported cost would have. A30 declares this intentional ("an upper bound"). Agree, recorded, no change.

## 3. NEW FINDINGS (this pass, on the fixed tree)

**N1 (real, minor, nobody): the cap-reached head can be missed when the route closes first** — as above. `downsignal.py:241-249`: the breach check is inside `if self.paid_open:`. Sequence: the last affordable call pushes `spent` past `cap − reserve`; the signal returns; the *next* `_route_tick` takes the close branch (downsignal.py:235-239) and never reaches the cap check; the head "MINER PAID ROUTE CAP REACHED" is never written for that day even though the cap was reached and the videos did wait. Evidence it matters: the ruling's own words make the cap a *switch-like* event ("Cap $2.00 vendor-reported per day on this route, then queue and wait"), and the skip reason *is* recorded per call (providers.py:862-864) but only in the calls log, not on STATE. Fix: run the cap check before the close branch, or also when `st["open"]` was just flipped False. P2.

**N2 (real, minor, nobody): `_route_tick`'s cap check uses `self.router.caps.today()`, which *rolls* the day — and the roll's loader reads the queue, so the first tick after ET midnight re-reads the day's spend; but the `paid_cap_day` kv (downsignal.py:246-247) is compared to *today's* date, so a cap reached yesterday does not suppress today's head.** Verified correct — I am recording it because it is the one place the ET-day roll (providers.py:697-701) and the kv's date string (downsignal.py:245) must agree, and they do: `datetime.fromtimestamp(now, ET).date().isoformat()` and `RoleCaps._roll`'s `datetime.fromtimestamp(self.clock(), ET).date()` are the same computation. No bug; noted so the next reviewer does not re-derive it.

**N3 (real, minor, nobody): a paid-route video that goes MINED_PARTIAL keeps `mine_route="mine_paid"` and `recheck_ts=NULL`, so it is re-queued for the Heimdall re-mine — and its *partial* Heimdall pass then sets `mine_route="mine"` (service.py:317, `_settle`'s `common`), which is correct — but if that Heimdall pass *also* fails partially, the video stays MINED_PARTIAL with `mine_route="mine"` and `recheck_ts` set, so it is never re-queued again.** Trace: `_queue_rechecks` (service.py:586-588) sets `recheck_ts=self.clock()` at queueing time, not at completion; `recheck_due` (queue.py:191) requires `recheck_ts IS NULL`. So one re-mine opportunity per paid-route video, taken at queueing time. If that one Heimdall pass fails (outage — Heimdall was just down, after all), the video's paid-route rows keep their labels, the re-mine is never retried, and the video sits in MINED_PARTIAL retried every MINE_PARTIAL_RETRY_S (service.py:338) *without* the `mine_route`/`recheck_ts` machinery ever firing again — because `recheck_due` needs `mine_route="mine_paid"` and `_settle` just overwrote it to "mine". Is that a loss? The paid rows are in the ledger (the receipt), the partial-retry loop keeps trying the *whole video* on Heimdall anyway (MINE_DUE includes MINED_PARTIAL, queue.py:25), so the moments *do* get a Heimdall pass eventually — just via the partial-retry path, not the recheck path, and without the "once" guarantee A31 promised. Actually, reading A31 again: "mined again on the mine role, once" — the once is satisfied. The flags: `carry_forward` (ledger.py:187-209) unions whatever the later pass finds. So: no loss, but the *mechanism* that guarantees the re-mine silently hands off to the partial-retry mechanism, and the two have different retry clocks (6 h vs. once). Worth a line in the STATE reason; not a defect. P2, recorded.

**N4 (verifiable, minor, nobody): `_paid_due`'s conversation sort is *unstable across ticks* in a way that can starve.** `vs.sort(key=lambda v: (conv(v), published_ts), reverse=True)` (service.py:577) — conversations first, then newest. But `due()` (queue.py:178-180) returns rows ordered by `COALESCE(published_ts, listing_ts, discovered_ts) DESC`, and `_paid_due` filters to 200. A conversation published 5.9 h ago sorts *ahead of* a conversation published 1 min ago? No — within conversations, `published_ts` DESC: newest conversation first. Correct. But a *non-conversation* published 1 min ago sorts behind a *conversation* published 5.9 h ago, and if the cap is reached after the first few, the fresh non-conversation waits the whole outage. That is exactly what A32 ordered ("conversations first, then newest" — *within* groups? The ruling says "newest first, conversations first", ambiguous). The code's reading (conversation-ness dominates recency) is defensible and matches the note in providers.json:99 ("conversations first (caption turns per minute >= conversation_tpm), then newest"). Agree with the code's reading; recording the ambiguity because the ruling's phrase "newest first, conversations first" could also be read as "newest first, *ties broken by* conversation-ness". If the orchestrator meant the latter, the sort key's order is swapped. P2, needs a ruling, not a code change.

**N5 (checked, correct, nobody): the hook pass during the paid route.** A28 says no hook-pass *re-runs*; the code's `hook_once` (service.py:417-438) is untouched by the diff and keeps mining hooks on the *hook* role (Heimdall) — so during the outage, hook passes fail with an outage and wait (service.py:389-394), catching up inside 12 h when Heimdall returns. Meanwhile the *moment* pass runs on the paid role. So a video mined on the paid route gets its hook rows only after Heimdall returns — and `hook_due` (queue.py:195-201) requires `state IN (MINED, MINED_PARTIAL)`, which the paid-route video reaches. Correct, and the 12 h hook horizon (service.py:59) exactly matches the moment expiry, so a paid-route video's hooks are never written for expired moments... unless the video was published 11.9 h ago, in which case its hooks are written just before expiry — the same edge as N3's re-mine, absorbed the same way. Verified; nobody (including me, in Cycle 1) traced the hook interaction. It is correct.

**N6 (new, small, nobody): `mine_video`'s cost query changed semantics.** service.py:271-272: `COALESCE(role, 'mine')=?` with `route` — for the paid route this sums only `role='mine_paid'` calls, correct. But the *cache* hits: a cached reply is emitted with `kind="cache"` and `cost_usd=0.0` (providers.py:907-909), so it adds nothing — right. And a *hook* pass's calls are excluded — right, they are role='hook'. But: the paid route's *re-mine* on the mine role later sums only role='mine' — so `mine_cost_usd` after the re-mine shows only the Heimdall pass's cost, and the earlier paid cost is forgotten in that column (it lives in the calls table). Cosmetic; the ledger's `mined.at`/`by` carries the truth. P2, cosmetic.

**N7 (checked, correct): the freeze lift cannot leak.** Every gate: `effective()` (providers.py:797-802, lifted = the *role's* own flag), `available(bid, breaker, lifted)` (providers.py:804-816, the flag travels from `run`'s `rc`), `_try`'s second lock (providers.py:917-918, reads `rc.get("freeze_lifted")` of the *role being run*). A role without `freeze_lifted` calling a frozen vendor: `run` computes `lifted` from its own role (providers.py:842), passes it to `available` — False → skipped. `_try` re-checks with its own role's rc. The only way a frozen vendor is called is through a role whose config names `freeze_lifted` AND has a cap (validate_config providers.py:128-129 enforces the pairing). Exactly one such role exists (mine_paid). Verified; this answers question 3 with a no.

**N8 (checked, correct): the ET-day roll at a restart.** Question 2's "the loader at a restart": `RoleCaps._roll` (providers.py:697-701) fires on the first `reserve`/`add`/`today` of a new day *or a fresh process* (day dict empty → `self.day.get(role) != d` → roll), and the loader (queue.py:182-186) sums `calls WHERE role=? AND ts >= start` where `start` is the ET midnight of the *current* ET day. A call that started before ET midnight and ended after counts once, on the day it *ended* (its `ts` is `self.clock()` at record time, queue.py:243) — matching the consensus's refutation of muse's midnight-leak. Verified.
