## Raised but not agreed
- qwen27b:F1 [critical] The second recogniser is called twice per span: once inside `pinned.segment_lock` (via the `recognise` callback) and again explicitly in `answer` for the `as_spoken` check. This violates the 'ONE paid reading per acquisition window' requirement and the test `test_one_paid_reading_per_acquisition` which expects `len(self.heard) == 1` for two spans. (agree: 0)
- qwen27b:F2 [high] The `spoken` text reconstruction is incorrect. It joins words from `sel` (the raw Whisper words) using indices from `spans` (the canonical spans). If a canonical token maps to multiple raw words (e.g., punctuation or contractions) or if `spans` indices don't align 1:1 with `sel` indices, the resulting `spoken` text will be malformed or missing words. Specifically, `spans[lo][1]` and `spans[hi-1][2]` are indices into the `spans` list, not necessarily valid indices into `sel` if the mapping isn't 1:1. (agree: 0)
- glm:F1 [medium] A paid second reading can be made more than once per acquisition window: second_reading caches by the source file's sha256, but the cache file lives in work_dir(vid, lo, hi, sha) — a per-request-window dir — while the ruling's 'ONE paid reading per acquisition window' and the test's two spans share one acquisition. If two spans of one acquisition resolve to different work dirs (or the cached sha key is absent, since provenance's source_file_sha256 may be missing, making the cached check c.get(...) == None pass/fail inconsistently), the second span pays again. The test test_one_paid_reading_per_acquisition only asserts len(self.heard)==1, which passes even if the second span's call were served from the decode cache rather than the reading cache, so it does not prove one paid call per window by the cache. (agree: 0)
