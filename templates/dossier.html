Brief 01 / 07 1100 – 1826 AD

Tally Sticks

England used split wooden sticks as money for over 700 years. Notches represented amounts. The stick split lengthwise: the creditor kept the stock (origin of stockholder), the debtor kept the foil (origin of counterfoil). No two wood grains match — counterfeiting was mathematically impossible. This was interest-free state money. From 1290–1485, laborers worked 14 weeks a year yet lived better than most do today. Then the Bank of England arrived in 1694 — a private cartel that hijacked the monetary system. Debt soared. Wars multiplied.

🪵
Key Metric
700 years of stable, interest-free money — ended by one private bank
The Math
0% interest vs 5–20% modern bank debt. Medieval peasants had more disposable time than their modern equivalents.
Technical Insight
Sound money creates prosperity. Private central banking creates debt slavery. The Bank of England was chartered the same year the British national debt was invented.

Brief 02 / 07 Ancient World – 1944

The Gold Standard

For millennia, gold served as honest money. Scarce. Durable. Divisible. Portable. Fungible. Nations pegged to gold were forced into fiscal discipline — you could not spend what you did not have. The classical gold standard (1870–1914) delivered unprecedented global trade, minimal inflation, and rising living standards for ordinary people. Gold's fatal flaw was political, not economic. Governments wanted to wage unlimited wars and build unlimited bureaucracies. Gold stood in their way.

🥇
Key Metric
1870–1914: ~0% inflation per year. 1971–2024: ~4% per year. Same math, different rulers.
The Math
44 years of classical gold standard: living standards doubled. Gold constrained governments. That is precisely why they abandoned it.
Technical Insight
Gold's fatal flaw: physical, nation-state-controlled, confiscatable. Bitcoin keeps gold's scarcity while removing every single one of its political vulnerabilities.
