## THE SCIENCE (not opinions — data)

### Attention Facts:
- 55% of viewers leave by the 60-second mark (2025 YouTube data)
- Viewers decide to stay or leave in 8 seconds
- AI-generated narration loses 35% more viewers in first 45 seconds vs human
- Pattern breaks create 15-22% re-engagement spikes
- Videos with 65%+ first-minute retention get 58% more total watch time
- For audiences 25+: visual changes every 15-25 seconds (NOT every 2 seconds)
- Over-editing DECREASES retention for mature audiences
- 45% of YouTube viewing now happens on TVs (longer attention spans)
- Sound design holds attention more than visuals alone
- Retention of 50-60% is solid; 70% earns priority recommendations

### The MrBeast Formula (adapted for intelligence content):
1. Instant stakes in first 5 seconds (what's at risk, what's happening)
2. Minute 0-1: Front-load the most interesting content
3. Minute 1-3: Crazy progression (cover ground fast, don't linger)
4. Minute 3: Re-engagement moment ("only Protocol Pulse can show you this")
5. Halfway point: New reason to keep watching (pivot, reveal, escalation)
6. Back half: Maintain quality, don't coast
7. End: Abrupt, high-energy. NEVER signal "we're wrapping up"
8. Payoff: The viewer should feel rewarded for staying

## SECTION 1: EPISODE STRUCTURE (12-15 minute daily brief)

### The Protocol Pulse Episode Arc:

```
[0:00-0:08]  COLD OPEN — The Hook
             Most shocking/interesting moment from today's intel.
             NO intro music. NO "welcome to Protocol Pulse."
             Start mid-action: "Bitcoin miners just lost $400 million
             in projected revenue overnight. Here's what happened."
             FACE ON SCREEN: Show the clip of the speaker saying it.

[0:08-0:30]  TITLE SEQUENCE — Remotion TitleCard (4 seconds)
             Animated logo + EKG pulse + episode title
             Music: 2 heartbeat thumps + synth tone
             Then IMMEDIATELY into content.

[0:30-2:00]  SEGMENT 1 — The Lead Story
             Partner clip (30-45 seconds of the best moment)
             Narrator analysis (30-45 seconds)
             During narration: show MUTED PREVIEW of upcoming Clip 2
             in a floating frame (picture-in-picture, 30% frame size,
             slight Ken Burns pan, bottom-right corner)

[2:00-2:03]  ALPHA TRANSITION — Glitch sweep + whoosh

[2:03-3:30]  SEGMENT 2 — The Escalation
             Second partner clip (different channel, different angle)
             Narrator connects it to Segment 1: "And this matters because..."
             During narration: previous clip thumbnail top-right corner
             (visual callback, "we just covered that")

[3:30-3:33]  TRANSITION + RE-ENGAGEMENT
             "But here's where it gets interesting..."
             This is the Minute 3 re-engagement from MrBeast's formula.
             The narrator teases what's coming without revealing it.

[3:33-5:30]  SEGMENT 3 — The Data Segment
             On-chain metrics, hash rate, price action, mining economics
             Visuals: Remotion data cards with animated numbers
             BTC price chart (static or animated)
             Narrator delivers in AUTHORITY voice mode

[5:30-5:33]  TRANSITION

[5:33-7:00]  SEGMENT 4 — The Wildcard
             The most unexpected or contrarian clip of the day
             Something that challenges the dominant narrative
             This is the "halfway re-engagement"

[7:00-7:03]  TRANSITION

[7:03-9:00]  SEGMENT 5 — WHAT THE BITCOIN INTERNET IS SAYING
             Social segment with cyberpunk tweet cards
             2-3 tweets with Remotion SocialCard visuals
             Narrator provides commentary on sentiment

[9:00-9:03]  TRANSITION

[9:03-10:30] SEGMENT 6 — The Deep Cut (optional, longer episodes)
             Fifth partner clip OR deeper analysis of lead story
             This is where the continuation series ties in

[10:30-11:30] WRAP — The Verdict
              Narrator delivers the takeaway
              "Here's what this means for your stack..."
              Voice mode: WARM
              Visual: Waveform with logo

[11:30-12:00] OUTRO — Branded video + CTA
              "Subscribe for tomorrow's brief"
              Protocol Pulse outro video
              NEVER say "thanks for watching" (signals ending, causes drop-off)
              End ABRUPTLY after CTA. No fade. No lingering.
```

### Segment Timing Rules:
- No single segment exceeds 2 minutes without a visual change
- Partner clips: 30-60 seconds (sweet spot: 40 seconds)
- Narrator segments: 30-60 seconds, ALWAYS with a visual element
  (preview clip, data card, social card, logo waveform — NEVER just waveform alone)
- Transitions: 1 second (current 0.7s is too fast, 1.5s is too slow)
- Total episode: 10-15 minutes for daily brief
- Shorts: 30-60 seconds, extracted from the single best moment

## SECTION 2: VISUAL PACING RULES (research-backed)

### The 15-25 Second Rule (for 25+ audience):
A visual change must occur every 15-25 seconds. NOT every 2 seconds
(that's for Gen Z TikTok; our audience is 25-55 Bitcoiners on desktop/TV).

Visual change means ANY of:
- Cut to partner clip
- Cut to narrator visual
- New data card appearing
- Social card entering
- Transition effect
- Camera angle shift within clip
- Picture-in-picture appearing/disappearing
- Lower third appearing
- Text overlay appearing

### The Narration Visual Stack:
When the narrator speaks, the screen is NEVER:
- Just a waveform on black (boring, AI-detected)
- Just text on black (worse)
- Static for more than 10 seconds

Instead, narration segments cycle through these layers:

**Layer 1 (background):** Cyberpunk animated loop (Remotion CyberpunkBackground)
**Layer 2 (primary visual):** ONE of these, rotating:
  - Muted preview of upcoming clip (PiP, 30% frame, panning)
  - Thumbnail of the clip just shown (top-right, with channel name)
  - Animated data card (BTC price, metric being discussed)
  - Social card (when discussing tweets)
**Layer 3 (brand):** Logo (transparent, subtle pulse) in corner
**Layer 4 (waveform):** Heartbeat waveform (bottom-third, mirrors speech)
**Layer 5 (info bar):** Gold bottom bar with BTC price, date, PROTOCOLPULSE.IO

This creates a rich, layered visual even without a face on screen.
The PiP preview clip is the most engaging option (face retention research).

### Color Pacing:
- Narration segments: Cooler tones (dark, cyberpunk, calm)
- Partner clips: Warmer tones (their natural lighting)
- Data segments: Higher contrast (darker blacks, brighter reds)
- Social segment: Neon-accented (red glow, scanlines)
- Transitions: Brief flash of pure red/white (pattern interrupt)

This temperature shift between segments acts as a subconscious visual cue
that something new is happening, preventing the "everything looks the same" fatigue.

## SECTION 3: SOUND DESIGN RULES

### Music:
- Intro: Full music, fade under narration within 5 seconds
- Under narration: -18dB to -22dB (present but not competing)
- Under partner clips: Music STOPS (let the clip's own audio breathe)
- Transitions: Brief music swell + whoosh
- Outro: Music fades up to full after last narrator word
- NEVER use the same track two days in a row
- Change track mood at halfway point (subtle shift, not jarring)

### Sound Effects (subtle, not overdone):
- Alpha transition: Whoosh + digital glitch (1 second)
- Social card entry: Soft swoosh (0.4 seconds, 50% volume of transition)
- Data card appearance: Subtle "blip" (0.2 seconds)
- Lower third: Quiet slide sound (0.3 seconds)
- BTC price flash: Subtle "ding" if price is up, muted "thud" if down

### Voice Dynamics:
- NEVER monotone for more than 15 seconds
- Speed: 1.10-1.15x default (slightly brisk, not rushed)
- Segment tags drive voice mode (PIPELINE_LAWS Section 14B)
- Pauses: 0.5-1 second pause before data reveals ("Hash rate just hit...
  [0.5s pause] ...one thousand and fifty-six exahash.")
- The pause before a number makes viewers lean in

## SECTION 4: THE FACE RULE

### Why faces matter:
- Thumbnails with faces get 38% higher CTR (YouTube internal data)
- Viewers retain attention 2x longer when a human face is on screen
- AI-only visuals trigger "AI detection" fatigue in 2026 viewers

### How Protocol Pulse uses faces:
1. **Partner clips**: Full screen, their face, their words. Maximum engagement.
2. **Narration PiP**: Muted preview of upcoming clip speaker (face visible)
3. **Social cards**: When tweet author has a recognizable avatar, show it
4. **Thumbnails**: ALWAYS include a face (partner speaker, Saylor, etc.)
5. **HeyGen avatar** (weekly PBX Report): Face on screen for full segment

### The PiP Preview Technique (from PBX):
During narration about upcoming clip:
- Show a 5-10 second MUTED clip of the upcoming speaker in a PiP frame
- Frame: luxurious thin border, slight drop shadow, rounded corners
- Size: 30% of frame, positioned bottom-right
- Effect: subtle Ken Burns pan (slow zoom or pan, keeps it alive)
- This serves three purposes:
  1. Face on screen = attention retention
  2. Preview creates curiosity gap ("who is this? what will they say?")
  3. Smooth visual transition from narration → full clip

## SECTION 5: THE ANTI-AI-DETECTION RULES

### The Problem:
2026 viewers have developed "AI slop radar." Content that looks or sounds
AI-generated gets abandoned 70% faster. Protocol Pulse must feel human-produced
even though the pipeline is autonomous.

### What triggers AI detection:
- Robotic or monotone narration (35% drop-off in first 45 seconds)
- Repetitive visual patterns (same layout for every segment)
- Generic stock imagery
- Perfect but lifeless animations (too smooth, no imperfections)
- Lack of a human face anywhere in the video
- Formulaic structure with no surprises

### How Protocol Pulse avoids AI detection:
1. **Voice variety**: 4 voice modes with natural energy shifts
2. **Human faces**: Partner clips + PiP previews ensure faces appear regularly
3. **Visual variety**: Every narration segment has a different primary visual
4. **Imperfections**: Occasional 1-frame glitch artifact in transitions (intentional)
5. **Sound design**: Whooshes, blips, music changes — sounds human-produced
6. **Personality**: Script writer adds dry humor, knowing observations, callbacks
7. **PBX voice clone** (when ready): The ultimate human anchor
8. **Varied pacing**: Some segments faster, some slower (not metronome-regular)

## SECTION 6: THUMBNAIL RULES

### The Formula:
- Face (50% of frame) — exaggerated expression from the featured speaker
- 3-5 words of text — the hook, not the topic
- Red accent — Protocol Pulse brand color as highlight
- High contrast — dark background, bright subject
- One clear focal point — viewer's eye knows where to look instantly

### Text Rules:
- Font: Bold sans-serif (Bebas Neue or Impact)
- Color: White with red accent on key word
- Max 5 words: "MINERS JUST LOST EVERYTHING" not "Bitcoin mining difficulty
  adjustment causes revenue decline for hash rate providers"
- Test 3 variants per episode, swap based on CTR data (V27)

## SECTION 7: PRODUCTION QUALITY BENCHMARKS

### Visual:
- Resolution: 1920x1080 minimum, always
- Bitrate: 8Mbps target, 5Mbps floor
- Frame rate: 30fps CFR throughout
- Color: Consistent grade per segment type
- No visible compression artifacts on faces

### Audio:
- Narration: -14 LUFS (broadcast standard)
- Music bed: -30 to -36 LUFS under narration
- Partner clips: normalized to -14 LUFS
- No clipping, no distortion, no background noise
- AV sync: < 0.03s on final output

### Pacing:
- No static visual for more than 10 seconds
- Visual change every 15-25 seconds
- Episode duration: 10-15 minutes
- No segment exceeds 2 minutes
