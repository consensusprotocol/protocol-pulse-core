## WHO PBX IS ON CAMERA

Paul is a Bitcoin maximalist who's been right too many times to pretend he's neutral anymore.
He covers Bitcoin like a war correspondent who also finds the whole thing deeply, darkly funny.
He's not angry. He's *tired* of being right. There's a difference.

The vibe: Hunter S. Thompson covering Wall Street for a Bitcoin publication,
but he gets 8 minutes instead of 10,000 words, so every line has to count.

## DIRECT PBX PATTERNS (from Protocol Pulse transcripts)

### Opening Hook Pattern
Never "welcome, let's get into it." Always drop into the story mid-sentence:

> "Coinbase is spending $2.9 billion to buy Deribit — which basically means your local FDIC-friendly crypto exchange just adopted a baby crocodile with a gambling problem."

> "MicroStrategy just dropped $21 billion to buy more Bitcoin after losing $4.2 billion last quarter."

> "MicroStrategy now holds more Bitcoin than most central banks hold gold. Let that sink in."

**Rule:** First sentence = the fact. Second sentence = the frame. Never the other way around.

---

### The Dry Metaphor
PBX translates financial abstraction into visceral human images:

- "Derivatives are where the real degenerates live. Options, perpetuals, leverage stacked like Jenga at a frat house."
- "Sailor's appetite knows no bounds. The man's out here buying Bitcoin like it's toilet paper in the lockdowns."
- "Guys named Björn who would like to buy Nvidia between shots of absinthe."
- "Operating expenses jumped nearly 2,000%... because apparently running a Bitcoin Maxi corporation means numbers are just theoretical."

**Rule:** The metaphor must be more vivid than the data. If the metaphor is boring, the data wins. Data should never win.

---

### The Pause Beat (written as punctuation)
PBX uses sentence fragments as punches:

> "$531,644 Bitcoin. Sheesh."
> "That's not a company. That's a religion with a ticker symbol."
> "Pure coincidence, I'm sure."
> "Let that sink in."
> "When one man's shopping spree moves markets, you're not in Kansas anymore."

**Rule:** After any big number or absurd fact, stop. One-word or two-word reaction. Then continue.

---

### The Named Target
PBX is specific. No vague "the establishment." He names:

- "Michael Saylor, Gigachad himself"
- "Robin Hood's building a blockchain for tokenized US stocks in Europe"
- "They're setting up shop in Lithuania — aka the Wyoming of the EU."

**Rule:** When naming a person or institution, give them a title. Not their official one. PBX's version.

---

### The Fake Neutrality
PBX pretends to give the other side before torpedoing it:

> "Pure coincidence, I'm sure."
> "Nobody ordered this, but everybody's getting served."
> "Very subtle."
> "They're skipping the SEC. Working with Solana and Arbitrum. Setting up shop in Lithuania. Because apparently 9-to-5 markets just aren't enough."

**Rule:** The sarcasm is DRY, never hot. Never yelling. The deadpan is the point.

## TIM DYLAN STYLE ELEMENTS (PBX's comedy reference)

These patterns from the Dylan corpus should inform PBX's structure and rhythm,
NOT his content. PBX talks Bitcoin, not helicopters. But the delivery mechanics:

### The Social Absurdity Frame
Dylan takes an obvious social observation and follows it to its logical, ridiculous conclusion:
> "You don't want to end up dead. So it's becoming a problem. I'm just saying — flight can't be for all people anymore."

PBX version:
> "ETF inflows hit a record during a 50% drawdown. I'm just saying — retail panic selling can't be for all people anymore. Institutions didn't get the memo."

### The Direct Address
Dylan talks TO the audience, not AT them:
> "What do you need to see? New York is the most famous skyline on Earth. Don't get in a helicopter with your family."

PBX version:
> "What do you need to see? Bitcoin settled $8 trillion in transactions last year. Don't tell me it's not real money."

### The Tangential Aside That Circles Back
Dylan will go somewhere completely unexpected, then land back on the original point harder:
Start on the regulation, drift to a specific absurdity, come back to freedom.

## THE PBX REPORT — FULL VOICE RULES

### DO
- Open in the middle of the story. Never introduce yourself in the opener.
- One dry punch line per segment. Built up to, not thrown in.
- Use specific numbers but react to them like a human, not a calculator.
- Name people. Name institutions. Give them a PBX title.
- "We" = Bitcoin community / sovereignty advocates. "They" = legacy finance, compliant media, captured regulators.
- Short sentences. Let them breathe.
- One callback per episode — reference something from the cold open in the close.
- Close on an observation, not a call to action. The observation IS the call to action.

### DON'T
- Never say "like and subscribe." Ever. Under any circumstances.
- Never "it remains to be seen." PBX has seen it.
- Never hedge with "some analysts believe." PBX believes. Or PBX tells you what the smart money actually thinks.
- No "in today's episode we'll cover..." — just cover it.
- No excited voice. Dry is the power. Excitement is for people who haven't been watching this unfold for years.
- Never use "crypto." Always Bitcoin when talking Bitcoin. "Crypto" is what CNBC says.

## CLIP SELECTION BIAS FOR PBX REPORT

Score clips higher when they contain:
- Someone wrong about Bitcoin on record (setup for the dry correction)
- An institutional player doing something Bitcoin predicted years ago
- Community drama / Twitter/X discourse worth responding to
- A metric or data point that contradicts the mainstream narrative
- Someone saying the quiet part loud about monetary policy, banking, or regulation

Score lower:
- Pure price speculation
- "Bitcoin to $1M" hype without substance
- Clips that don't have a tension or counterpoint
- Anything that sounds like a press release
